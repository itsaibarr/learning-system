# Evidence map: do world models understand when physics breaks?

Assembled 2026-09-02 from a 15-agent research pass, every load-bearing claim attacked by an
independent adversarial verifier. Many figures below were **recomputed from Meta's released
per-video CSVs** (`facebookresearch/jepa-intuitive-physics`) rather than taken from paper
tables — where those disagree, the recomputation is noted.

## Verdict

On the narrowest reading — does some readout of some model separate matched possible from impossible video above chance — the answer is yes, and it is reproducible: a frozen V-JEPA ViT-H's representation-space prediction error ranks the impossible member of an IntPhys-dev quadruplet correctly 98.33% of the time against 50% chance and a 51.4% untrained-network control, a figure that has been recomputed independently from the authors' own released per-video CSVs using their own aggregation function. On every reading stronger than that, the evidence does not currently support a yes. The same metric on the same model falls to ~66% on GRASP and ~62% on InfLevel-lab, and the per-property recomputation shows those aggregates are carried by a few blocks while InfLevel gravity (50.4 vs 51.0 untrained) and solidity (51.4 vs 51.9) are complete nulls and GRASP colour constancy is systematically inverted at longer contexts; the same lab's harder benchmark, IntPhys 2, reports most models between 50% and ~57.5% where humans are near-perfect; absolute single-video accuracy collapses to 68% on IntPhys and ~52% elsewhere even with a threshold calibrated on the test set's own real videos; and the headline aggregates are a per-property maximum over six context-length settings, an inflation that also lifts the untrained control from ~49% to 55.9%. Two structural features mean the surviving above-chance signal cannot be attributed to physics without further work: IntPhys trains on possible events only, so impossible transitions are out-of-distribution by construction and prediction error is the standard novelty signal; and a likelihood-shaped score has no modal category, so it can represent "rarely happens" but not "cannot happen" — exactly the contrast on which language models tested directly are unreliable and sometimes below chance. Two 2026 results cut in opposite directions and both weaken the simple story: plausibility is linearly decodable at ~81% from video-diffusion internals whose own generations violate the same laws (so output-based measures understate what is represented), while five training-free kinematic statistics on frozen non-video image encoders are reported at 93.3% on IntPhys 2 (so detection may not require a world model at all). The honest formulation is that representation-space surprise is a real, readout-dependent signal that survives the field's best low-level control — the pixel-matched quadruplet — on its easiest, most synthetic benchmark, and does not survive photorealism, real lab video, absolute calibration, or several individual principles; that is what an anomaly detector with a good temporal prior looks like, and it is necessary but nowhere near sufficient for understanding. Finally, Mitchell and Krakauer's point is load-bearing here rather than decorative: "understands physics" is not one question, and disaggregated into detection, calibrated modal judgement, explanation, counterfactual competence and control, the current evidence supports the first, is silent or negative on the second and third, has never been tested on the fourth, and is suggestive but unquantified on the fifth. 

## The distinction to build on

Higher prediction error on an impossible video establishes a graded, within-stimulus-set, readout-dependent contrast — "this is the odd one out among these four clips, under this error space, this context length and this aggregation" — whereas "understands when physics breaks" asserts a modal, absolute, transferable judgement: "this cannot happen, whatever else is on the screen." Every element of that gap is separately measurable and every one currently fails or is untested. Modal vs graded: a likelihood-shaped score has no category for impossibility, only a low-probability region, and models tested directly on impossible-vs-improbable are unreliable and sometimes below chance. Absolute vs relative: IntPhys defines both metrics precisely because plausibility scores are uncalibrated across scene difficulty, and V-JEPA's 98.3% relative becomes 68.1% absolute on the same items and ~52% absolute on GRASP and InfLevel — and humans themselves lose 8-17 points making the same move, so absolute failure is partly a property of the protocol. Transferable vs stimulus-bound: the same paradigm yields 98% on synthetic IntPhys and ~53% on photorealistic IntPhys 2. And readout-independent vs readout-dependent: identical architecture and data score 91.7% predicting in representation space and 58.9% predicting in pixels, the aggregate moves ~10 points depending on whether you take a max over context lengths, and average-vs-max aggregation flips conclusions — so "surprise" is a family of instruments, not a reading of the model's beliefs. The paper should therefore never write "the model detects the violation"; it should write "under readout R, on stimulus distribution D, the model's prediction error orders these matched videos correctly at rate p" — and then argue about which of the four gaps a given experiment closes. 

## Evidence FOR

### [strong] A frozen, zero-shot V-JEPA ViT-H (RoPE, VideoMix2M) scores 98.33% relative pairwise accuracy on IntPhys-dev (per-block O1 100.0 / O2 100.0 / O3 95.0), against 50% chance and a 20-seed untrained ViT-L control at 51.44 +/- 0.67. No probe, no fine-tuning, no physics supervision: the classifier is the sign of a surprise difference within a pixel-matched pair.

- Source: Garrido, Ballas, Assran, Bardes, Najman, Rabbat, Dupoux, LeCun, arXiv:2502.11831; recomputed from the authors' released per-video CSVs in facebookresearch/jepa-intuitive-physics (data_intphys.tar.gz) using their own get_average_acc aggregation
- Why that strength: The digits were reproduced independently, twice, from the authors' primary released artifact rather than from the paper's tables, and the untrained-network control rules out the weakest deflationary story (that architecture-induced low-level sensitivity alone separates the classes). Strong for exactly this narrow claim and no wider: it is the dev split, GRASP is level-2 only, and each block's score is a max over six context settings.

### [strong] The prediction target, not the data or the scale, is what produces the effect: an otherwise identical V-JEPA-L trained to predict in PIXEL space scores 58.89% on IntPhys against 91.67% for its representation-space twin, and the gap is more robust at fixed context (~51-56% vs ~86-91%) than in the max-selected aggregate. VideoMAEv2-g, an independent 1B-parameter pixel-reconstruction model, corroborates at 59.44%.

- Source: Recomputed from data_intphys/vit-l-rope-howto-pixels vs vit-l-rope-howto and videomaev2_g in facebookresearch/jepa-intuitive-physics
- Why that strength: This is a matched ablation — same architecture, same HowTo100M data, same evaluation — with only the prediction space changed, and it reproduces from primary data and holds up better without max-over-context selection than with it. Two caveats: 'otherwise identical' is false at the evaluation level (the pixel model was given an extra C=12 context setting and still lost), and it is one seed of one ablation pair on ViT-L, not the headline ViT-H.

### [moderate] The effect is cheap and saturates early: a ViT-L trained on 0.1% of HowTo100M (~129 hours, ~5.4 days of unique video) already reaches 75.6% on IntPhys, 1% reaches 94.4%, and a 115M-parameter ViT-B reaches 90.6% — with the data curve flat-to-non-monotonic above 1% (94.4 / 95.0 / 92.2 / 93.9 / 91.7 at 1/10/25/50/100%).

- Source: Recomputed from the vit-l-rope-howto-{0_1p,1p,10p,25p,50p}_vid and vit-{b,l,h}-rope-howto series in facebookresearch/jepa-intuitive-physics; the seconds-to-days conversion constant is hard-coded in the authors' figures.ipynb
- Why that strength: Reproducible from primary data and it grounds the paper's 'core knowledge need not be hardwired' framing. But it is IntPhys-only, and the flat curve above 1% cuts the other way: if a 115M model on five days of video saturates the benchmark, the benchmark rather than the model may be what is being measured — which is why this belongs in the 'for' column only with that sentence attached.

### [moderate] Pretraining distribution and masking strategy matter in ways a pure novelty story does not obviously predict: on IntPhys, Something-Something-v2 alone gives 46.7% (below chance, two of three blocks well below), Kinetics-710 78.3%, HowTo100M 91.7%, VideoMix2M 95.0%; and the block-causal masking variant — the pretraining regime closest to the causal temporal mask used at evaluation — is the WORST of four (78.3 vs 95.0 for plain block masking).

- Source: Recomputed from data_intphys/vit-l-rope-{ssv2,k710,howto,vm2m,k710-ssv2} and vit-l-rope-vm2m-{random_mask,tube_mask,block_causal}
- Why that strength: Reproduced from primary data, and the masking result is genuine evidence against the simplest deflationary alternative (that the effect is train/test mask alignment). It is only moderate because it shows the effect is contingent on the pretraining distribution — which is equally consistent with 'the model learned the statistics of the right kind of video' as with 'the model learned physics'.

### [weak] Physical plausibility is linearly decodable at ~81.27% average accuracy from the intermediate states of a video diffusion transformer across IntPhys and InfLevel — beating dedicated representation-learning baselines including V-JEPA 2 — while the same models generate video violating the laws they evidently encode; the signal is absent from the VAE latent and emerges inside the denoising transformer.

- Source: Esmati, Nath, Hofmann, Nowrouzezahrai, Ebrahimi Kahou, Mirmehdi, 'The Invisible Hand of Physics', arXiv:2606.05328
- Why that strength: Known only from search summaries — the PDF was never reached, the probe train/test split and the cross-benchmark averaging are unverified, and the V-JEPA comparison number may be quoted from a different protocol. It is also a TRAINED PROBE, which InfLevel's authors forbid on the explicit ground that a probe confounds physical understanding with feature extraction, and which Vafa et al.'s inductive-bias result argues is too cheap a criterion. Its real value is as a representation-vs-behaviour dissociation, not as evidence of understanding.

### [weak] Frozen-feature probing across model families finds physics information weakest in early layers and most accessible at intermediate-to-late depth, with V-JEPA strongest — and, critically, temporal controls bite: shuffling frame order cuts pair consistency by 39-61% under MLP probes and single-frame repetition by 86-100%, with an attentive probe on LTX-Video collapsing 96-97%.

- Source: Punzo, Caselli, Pantelidis, Massafra, Lo Sardo, Salehi, 'Do Video Foundation Models Understand Intuitive Physics? A Layerwise Probing Analysis', arXiv:2606.09646
- Why that strength: Unread 2026 preprint known through summaries, and probe-based rather than zero-shot so its numbers are not comparable to the 98/66/62 headline. But the frame-shuffle and single-frame ablations are the only published temporal controls I have, and they do argue that a purely static-appearance shortcut is not what is being read out — which is a genuine, if partial, answer to the deflationary challenge and simultaneously quantifies how much of a physical-video score can be single-frame shortcut (near-total on MVP).

### [moderate] A surprise measure CAN index physics rather than novelty when it comes from a structured generative model: ADEPT computes surprise as a likelihood under a particle-filtered probabilistic physics engine over coarse ellipsoid object representations and is reported to discriminate implausible scenes at roughly human level, validated against graded human expectation ratings rather than binary labels.

- Source: Smith, Mei, Yao, Wu, Spelke, Tenenbaum, Ullman, ADEPT (NeurIPS 2019); Smith & Ullman et al., 'The fine structure of surprise in intuitive physics', CogSci 2020
- Why that strength: This is the existence proof that matters methodologically: ADEPT's surprise is a likelihood under an explicit physics model, not a reconstruction error over a training distribution, so it does not inherit the out-of-distribution novelty confound in the same way, and it is validated against graded human surprise rather than a binary target. Moderate rather than strong because it is not a learned world model from video — the physics is supplied — so it tells you what a physics-indexed surprise would look like, not that any video model has one.

## Evidence AGAINST

### [strong] The relative result does not transfer to absolute judgement. V-JEPA ViT-H scores 68.06% absolute (single-video) accuracy on IntPhys against 98.33% relative, and only 52.0% on GRASP and 52.8% on InfLevel-lab, versus untrained controls at ~50.0-50.7%. With an oracle threshold swept to maximise test accuracy it reaches 79.7 / 56.3 / 54.6. The 'calibrated' threshold is itself generous: it is set at the 90th percentile of the surprise of the real videos in the same evaluation block, i.e. fitted on test data with a 90% true-positive rate on possible videos guaranteed by construction.

- Source: Recomputed from eval.py compute_metrics and the performance.csv files in facebookresearch/jepa-intuitive-physics
- Why that strength: Reproduced from the authors' own released data and code, and it is the single cleanest operationalisation of the paper's central distinction: outside IntPhys the model cannot say whether one video is physically possible, only which of two matched videos is the odd one out. The one hedge that must travel with it is that humans also lose 8-17 points moving from relative to absolute on IntPhys, so absolute failure is partly a protocol property — which is exactly why the model-vs-human GAP profile, not the raw drop, is the informative quantity.

### [strong] Broken down by physical property against 20 untrained controls, the effect is present on some principles and absent or inverted on others. InfLevel gravity 50.4 +/- 0.4 vs untrained 51.0 +/- 1.5, and solidity 51.4 +/- 1.3 vs 51.9 +/- 1.7 — complete nulls with tiny variance for trained and untrained alike. GRASP colour constancy 58.1 vs 63.9 untrained, i.e. below random init. And on GRASP 'Unchangeableness' the signal is systematically INVERTED at longer contexts (0.00-5.47% accuracy for vit-h-rope-howto), meaning the model is reliably MORE surprised by the physically legal video — a consistent sign error, not noise, hidden by the max-over-contexts aggregation.

- Source: Per-property recomputation from the released performance.csv files in facebookresearch/jepa-intuitive-physics
- Why that strength: Recomputed from primary data with the authors' own category mapping and their own untrained-control set. An inverted signal is more informative than a null one and no published analysis explains it; and the fact that the reported category bar (58-65%) conceals a 0-5% sub-signal is a direct demonstration that the aggregation is doing argumentative work. Crucially, it also means aggregate 'the model understands intuitive physics' claims are not principle-indexed and should not be made.

### [strong] The headline GRASP and InfLevel numbers are inflated by post-hoc per-property selection of the best context length, and the untrained baseline is inflated by the same selection. V-JEPA-H's GRASP average is 51.1-57.5% at any single fixed context versus 66.36% after the max; InfLevel 57.6-60.1% versus 61.53%. The 20 untrained ViT-L controls go from 48.7-49.7% at fixed context to 55.9% after the identical max — so the plotted 'untrained networks' bar is itself partly a selection artifact and the true fixed-context floor is ~49-50%.

- Source: Recomputed per fixed context length from the released performance.csv files in facebookresearch/jepa-intuitive-physics
- Why that strength: Reproduced digit for digit from primary data, including the counter-intuitive control inflation, which is the load-bearing part. The aggregation is applied uniformly to every model so it is an optimistic protocol rather than a V-JEPA-specific favour — but it means the defensible GRASP effect is roughly 8 points over control at fixed context, not the ~10.5 implied by 66.4 vs 55.9, and it makes a held-out context-selection protocol a precondition for any single-number claim.

### [strong] The same violation-of-expectation logic, from an overlapping team, collapses on harder scenes: IntPhys 2's official README states verbatim that 'most models perform[] at chance levels (50%), in stark contrast to human performance, which achieves near-perfect accuracy', with humans reported at 96.44% overall and 92.44% held-out, best MLLM Gemini 2.5 Flash ~55.6% and best prediction model V-JEPA 2 ~57.5%. The README's sentence exempts nobody — it indicts prediction-based world models alongside MLLMs.

- Source: Bordes, Garrido, Kao, Williams, Rabbat, Dupoux, arXiv:2506.09849; README verbatim from facebookresearch/IntPhys2
- Why that strength: Strong for the qualitative claim, which was read verbatim from the official repository of the same lab that published the positive result — a lab publishing its own deflationary follow-up is the hardest kind of evidence to dismiss as motivated. Weaker on the digits: the per-model percentages and the human baselines could not be verified against the PDF in this corpus, and the reported per-split figures are a max over prompt variants, seeds, frame rates and camera conditions with unparseable answers scored as errors.

### [weak] Detection on these suites may not require a world model at all: five training-free kinematic statistics (speed variation, curvature, angle consistency, acceleration, linear-prediction residual) computed on frozen DINOv2 / DINOv3 / CORnet-S / VOneNet per-frame features — none pretrained on video or physics, zero learned parameters — are reported at 93.3% on IntPhys 2 and 98.3% on LikePhys where V-JEPA 2, GPT-4o, Gemini and twelve video diffusion models sit near chance.

- Source: Internò, Pondaven, Issa, Pizzati, Pinto, Olhofer, Laptev, Torr, Simoncelli, Hammer, Klindt, GeoPhys, arXiv:2606.20707 / github.com/christianinterno/GeoPhys
- Why that strength: Single very recent preprint, README only, no result tables, code unreleased at the time of reading, the IntPhys 2 split for 93.3% unstated, and the near-chance baselines are a competing-method claim nobody has independently checked. It is nonetheless the sharpest existing threat to the paper's own thesis and must be engaged rather than footnoted: if a trajectory-smoothness heuristic over static image features beats every world model at flagging violations, then 'the model registers the violation' cannot by itself be evidence of physical understanding — and no benchmark in the landscape currently ships an adversarial control designed to defeat such a detector, though InfLevel's README anticipates exactly this failure mode.

### [strong] Video generation models do not abstract physical rules where the training distribution can be controlled: in a deterministic 2D testbed, in-distribution velocity error falls to 0.012 against a 0.010 ground-truth-parse floor while out-of-distribution error stays ~0.427, is 'highly random' rather than improving with data (DiT-B: 0.433 / 0.328 / 0.358 at 30K/300K/3M), and DiT-XL shows no improvement. Generalisation is 'case-based' — mimicking the closest training example — with an observed attribute priority color > size > velocity > shape, i.e. the model sacrifices a dynamical variable to preserve a static appearance variable.

- Source: Kang, Yue, Lu, Lin, Zhao, Wang, Huang, Feng, arXiv:2411.02385 (ICML 2025); phyworld.github.io
- Why that strength: Verbatim from the authors' project page and a controlled synthetic design that separates in-distribution, combinatorial and out-of-distribution regimes — the only place in this literature where the training distribution is fully known. Two required hedges: combinatorial generalisation DOES scale (out-of-template abnormal rate 67% -> 10% as templates go 6 -> 60), so 'no abstraction at all' overstates; and the pixel/VAE-latent-distance explanation for the priority ordering is offered by the authors as an unmeasured hypothesis in an 'Open Discussion' section, with the ring-vs-ball control falsifying the ordering's generality while the authors read it as validating their hypothesis.

### [moderate] Sequence-prediction accuracy and world-model recovery dissociate: foundation models trained on orbital trajectories excel at their training task yet 'consistently fail to apply Newtonian mechanics when adapted to new physics tasks', behaving as if they had developed task-specific heuristics that do not generalise. Separately, models that pass existing world-model diagnostics show far less coherent world models under Myhill-Nerode state-equivalence metrics, and that incoherence produces failures on related but subtly different tasks.

- Source: Vafa, Chang, Rambachan, Mullainathan, arXiv:2507.06952; Vafa, Chen, Rambachan, Kleinberg, Mullainathan, arXiv:2406.03689
- Why that strength: Abstracts confirmed verbatim from multiple independent mirrors and the argument transfers directly: it is the strongest existing rebuttal to 'the probe found it, therefore the model has it'. Moderate rather than strong for three reasons: the venues could not be verified against primary proceedings; the tested systems are orbital-trajectory and DFA-domain sequence models, not video world models, so the transfer to intuitive physics is an analogy nobody has run; and a 2026 follow-up argues the Kepler-to-Newton failure is partly an artifact of tokenisation, with a Newtonian world model emerging at block size 2.

### [moderate] Visual realism and physical accuracy are decoupled: on a score normalised so that a second real take of the same scenario equals 100, the best model in the Physics-IQ paper reached 29.5% and Sora scored 10.0% while being the most visually deceptive model tested (55.6% on an MLLM 2AFC where 50% is chance). Even in 2026 the best VERIFIED entry is 58.2 +/- 1.8, and it uses best-of-N reranking against an external verifier; the best single-model image-to-video entry is 39.5 +/- 0.8.

- Source: Motamed, Culp, Swersky, Jaini, Geirhos, arXiv:2501.09038 (WACV 2026); leaderboards read directly from github.com/google-deepmind/physics-IQ-benchmark
- Why that strength: The leaderboards were read directly from the official repository and the ceiling is defined by real-world repeatability rather than by a human judgement, which is unusually clean. Moderate because the reported realism-physics correlation (r = -0.46, p = .249) came from a search summary rather than the PDF; because Original and Verified protocols are not comparable (the audit revised 57.6% of samples and produced Kendall tau 0.46 ranking changes); and because Physics-IQ scores generation, not detection, so it constrains rather than settles the detection question. Note also the realism judge is Gemini 1.5 Pro, which is itself below chance on IntPhys — a physics-blind judge measuring realism.

### [moderate] Frontier VLMs show the exact failure signature the question is about: they accept real video and do not flag impossible video. Gemini 2.5 Pro flags 41.3% of implausible videos while accepting 100.0% of real ones; GPT-4o 32.7% vs 84.7%; pretrained LLaVA-NeXT 3.3% vs 98.7%. And the flag rate is partly a response threshold, not a percept: VLMs are reported to adopt a far more critical stance when told a video is AI-generated than when they presume it is real, while IPV-Bench reports F1 alongside accuracy precisely because models answer Yes (or No) monotonically.

- Source: ImplausiBench/TRAVL, Motamed, Chen, Van Gool, Laina, arXiv:2510.07550 (README table read directly); PhyDetEx; Bai, Ci, Shou, IPV-Bench, arXiv:2503.14378
- Why that strength: The ImplausiBench numbers were verified row by row against the authors' own README, and the asymmetry is the finding rather than the ranking. Moderate because there is no human solver baseline and no published chance floor for that benchmark, because the 'human/LLM' columns are judging protocols rather than human performance, and because the provenance-sensitivity result (PhyDetEx) could not be reached from any source — it is the only support for the register-versus-report distinction and is currently uncitable. If the response-bias effect is real, every accuracy number in this literature is confounded with criterion and nobody has decomposed d' from criterion.

### [strong] The infant literature the paradigm was borrowed from has not settled the confound in its favour: a pre-registered meta-analysis of 76 studies and 1,899 infants finds perceptual novelty and conceptual unexpectedness are independent, dissociable drivers of looking (age predicts response to unexpected events but not to visually novel ones; habituation predicts the reverse). A dynamic-field model with no concepts reproduces familiarity, novelty, intensity and age effects in looking; a full-factorial drawbridge with pupillometry concludes the object-permanence reading survives only if you ignore most cells of the design; and the method's validity is under live dispute (Paulus 2022 vs Stahl & Kibbe 2022).

- Source: Kunin, Piccolo, Saxe & Liu, Nature Human Behaviour 8(12):2342-2356 (2024); Schöner & Thelen, Psychological Review 113(2):273-299 (2006); Sirois & Jackson, Infancy 17:61-78 (2012); Paulus, ICD 31:e2306; Stahl & Kibbe, ICD 31(6):e2359
- Why that strength: The meta-analysis is pre-registered, large, and settles the 25-year dispute in a way that damages the import specifically: the confound is real and ADDITIVE, so it cannot be waved away by assertion and a valid instrument must dissociate the two components by design. Two hedges: Schöner & Thelen model habituation generally and do not simulate possible/impossible contrasts, so applying them to VoE is an extension; and the point is about what a looking-time (or surprise) difference licenses, not a claim that infants lack concepts.

### [strong] A likelihood-based score has no modal category. Language models almost always prefer possible over impossible in the easy case ('The teacher bought the laptop' vs 'The laptop bought the teacher') but show much less consistent preferences for likely vs unlikely events, and under certain conditions ALL models tested perform WORSE than chance, assigning higher probability to impossible sentences.

- Source: Michaelov, Estacio, Zhang & Bergen, 'Not quite Sherlock Holmes', Findings of ACL 2025 (arXiv:2506.06808)
- Why that strength: Peer-reviewed, tests exactly the improbable-vs-impossible contrast the whole question turns on, and reports below-chance behaviour rather than merely weak behaviour — which is hard to reconcile with a represented modal category. The limitation is modality: it is language, not video, so it establishes that a graded probability signal does not by itself constitute a modal representation, and leaves as an open experiment whether video surprise behaves the same way. That gap is precisely where the crux experiment sits.

### [weak] Data distribution is not the missing ingredient: pretraining V-JEPA on SAYCam — developmentally realistic egocentric child video, ~0.01% of the volume used by state-of-the-art models — yields no significant improvement on IntPhys 2, closing off the 'the models just need the right data' defence and pushing the explanation toward architecture, objective, or the instrument itself.

- Source: Su, Legris, Gureckis, Ren, 'Opinion: Learning Intuitive Physics May Require More than Visual Data', arXiv:2512.06232 (NeurIPS 2025)
- Why that strength: Known only through summaries; it is an opinion/position paper accepted at a workshop rather than a full empirical study; and it is a null result, which constrains rather than refutes. It earns a place because it forecloses the most common rescue of the negative results, and because it is independent of both Meta and the benchmark authors.

## The crux experiment — unrun

THE MATCHED-NOVELTY VoE TRIAD, with a heuristic null and a pre-registered readout grid. Nothing in the corpus dissociates "out-of-distribution transition" from "physically impossible transition" for a video model, because IntPhys and its descendants train on possible events only, which makes the two perfectly confounded by construction. Build the missing arm. For each impossible test clip, construct a THIRD clip that is physically POSSIBLE but matched to the impossible one on training-distribution novelty: a rare-but-legal trajectory, an object entering from an unattested direction, an unusual-but-solid collision outcome, a legal event under an odd camera or occluder schedule. Match novelty operationally rather than by intuition — pin the possible-but-novel clip to the impossible clip on a pre-committed novelty statistic computed from the training corpus (e.g. nearest-neighbour distance in the encoder's own feature space, or the surprise of an explicitly trained density model over video with no physical structure), and report the matching residual. The design is then a triad within each pixel-matched quadruplet family: familiar-possible, novel-possible, impossible — sharing prefixes and low-level statistics in the IntPhys manner, so the Bogartz/Sirois full-factorial demand is met on both axes at once. THE PREDICTION THAT SEPARATES THE HYPOTHESES: a novelty detector gives surprise(impossible) ~ surprise(novel-possible) > surprise(familiar-possible); a physics-sensitive model gives surprise(impossible) > surprise(novel-possible) ~ surprise(familiar-possible). Report the ratio [S(imp) - S(novel-poss)] / [S(novel-poss) - S(fam-poss)] as the headline statistic; that number, not a discrimination accuracy, is the thing the field currently lacks. ADD THREE ARMS THAT MAKE IT DECISIVE. (1) Novelty inoculation: continue pretraining on a held-in set of impossible transitions so they become in-distribution, then test structurally similar held-out impossible events. A physics-sensitive detector should retain the signal; a density model should lose it. This is the causal version of the correlational triad and nobody has run it. (2) A heuristic null, not just an untrained network: run a GeoPhys-style five-statistic trajectory-smoothness detector on frozen non-video image features, plus a per-frame-difference change-point detector, on identical stimuli. IntPhys quadruplets share a pixel-identical prefix and diverge at a known frame, so a pure change-point detector is a live alternative explanation for the 98%; if the heuristic null matches the world model on the triad, the world model has demonstrated nothing beyond it. (3) A pre-registered readout grid rather than a chosen readout: sweep error space (pixel / representation / layer index), context length M, horizon N, and aggregation (mean / max / percentile) on a fixed model, and report the full RANGE together with a held-out context-selection protocol — because the corpus already shows the max-over-context aggregation moves GRASP from ~51-57% to 66% and moves the untrained control from ~49% to 56%, and that pixel-vs-representation moves IntPhys from 59% to 92%. SCORING AND COMPARISON. Score everything under BOTH the relative and the absolute protocol with a threshold calibrated on held-out data rather than on the test block's own real videos, and index every result by principle rather than aggregating — continuity, solidity, permanence, shape constancy, gravity/support kept separate, because Spelke et al. (1992) report infants honouring continuity and solidity but not gravity and inertia in the tasks they ran, so an aggregate "below infant level" number is uninterpretable. Collect graded human surprise on the same triad (magnitude and onset time, per Smith/Ullman), and report the correlation between the model's per-frame surprise trace and the human graded trace, not merely the sign of the pairwise contrast. WHY THIS IS PUBLISHABLE AS A CONTRIBUTION: it is the ML analogue of the Event Set x Event Set design the infant critics demanded in 1997 and of the improbable/impossible/inconceivable dissociation that has been run on language models but not on video; it produces a single interpretable ratio in place of a protocol-bound accuracy; it ships an adversarial control against the trajectory-smoothness heuristic that no current benchmark includes; and the raw per-video surprise tensors and evaluation code for the flagship model are already publicly released, so the readout grid and the per-frame traces can be computed before a single new stimulus is rendered. 

## Gaps this paper could claim

- No experiment anywhere in the corpus separates 'unseen spatiotemporal transition' from 'physically impossible transition' for a video model. The pixel-matched quadruplet equates static appearance and motion magnitude but leaves the transition type perfectly confounded with training-distribution novelty, and no benchmark ships an unfamiliar-but-possible control. Building it is a contribution in itself, and the framing of prediction error as the standard novelty signal is currently the paper's own synthesis rather than a citable objection — either find a source or claim it.

## Reading order

1. **Riochet, Castro, Bernard, Lerer, Fergus, Izard & Dupoux, 'IntPhys: A Framework and Benchmark for Visual Intuitive Physics Reasoning', arXiv:1803.07616 (2018)** — Read first because it defines the measurement everything else argues about: the possible-only training set, the pixel-matched quadruplet (the field's one real low-level control, and the ML analogue of the factorial design the infant critics demanded), and the relative-vs-absolute protocol split that the rest of the literature conflates. Verify the per-block violation wording and the dataset sizes against the PDF — the '3,600 movies per block' figure circulating in secondary sources inflates the whole test set threefold.  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07616
2. **Garrido, Ballas, Assran, Bardes, Najman, Rabbat, Dupoux & LeCun, 'Intuitive physics understanding emerges from self-supervised pretraining on natural videos', arXiv:2502.11831 — and the released code and per-video data at facebookresearch/jepa-intuitive-physics** — The strongest positive result and the only one you can audit yourself: clone the repo, extract data_intphys.tar.gz, and re-run their get_average_acc from figures.ipynb. Doing this before you read the paper's prose is what surfaces the qualifications that decide the argument — dev split, GRASP level-2 only, per-property max over six context settings, absolute accuracy at 68% where relative is 98%, and gravity and solidity pinned to the untrained control.  
   https://github.com/facebookresearch/jepa-intuitive-physics
3. **Bordes, Garrido, Kao, Williams, Rabbat & Dupoux, 'IntPhys 2: Benchmarking Intuitive Physics Understanding In Complex Synthetic Environments', arXiv:2506.09849 (2025)** — Read immediately after, because it is the same paradigm from an overlapping team on photorealistic scenes with most models at chance and humans near-perfect — the single most damaging datapoint for reading surprise as a transferable competence. Extract the per-model and per-split tables and the human baseline directly; and note the scoring notebook takes a max over prompts, seeds, frame rates and camera conditions and counts unparseable answers as errors.  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09849
4. **Weihs, Yuile, Baillargeon, Fisher, Marcus, Mottaghi & Kembhavi, 'Benchmarking Progress to Infant-Level Physical Reasoning in AI' (InfLevel), TMLR (2022)** — Next, for the prior negative result (ten architectures, ~60 experiments, at or near chance on continuity, solidity and gravity) and for the most important methodological statement in the whole landscape: InfLevel forbids training, fine-tuning and even linear probing on the explicit ground that a probe confounds physical understanding with feature extraction. That rule is what tells you where to put the probing results in your argument. Baillargeon's co-authorship also matters rhetorically.  
   https://openreview.net/forum?id=9NjqD9i48M
5. **Kunin, Piccolo, Saxe & Liu, 'Perceptual and conceptual novelty independently guide infant looking behaviour', Nature Human Behaviour 8(12):2342-2356 (2024)** — Now go to the source discipline: 76 studies, 1,899 infants, pre-registered, showing perceptual novelty and conceptual unexpectedness are independent and additive drivers of looking, dissociated by age and by habituation. This is what licenses your central methodological claim — that an ML VoE score must dissociate the two BY DESIGN — and it also hands you the moderator template (pretraining scale as the age proxy, scene-distribution exposure as the habituation proxy) for a model-side analogue.  
   https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01965-x
6. **Michaelov, Estacio, Zhang & Bergen, 'Not quite Sherlock Holmes: Language model predictions do not reliably differentiate impossible from improbable events', Findings of ACL 2025** — Read this for the sharpest statement of the modal problem in a model: likelihood separates possible from impossible in the easy case but not likely from unlikely, and under some conditions every model tested goes below chance on impossible sentences. It is the reason a graded surprise score cannot be read as 'the model knows this cannot happen' — and it is the experiment your paper should port from text to video.  
   https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.696/
7. **Hu, Sosa & Ullman, 'Shades of Zero: Distinguishing impossibility from inconceivability', Journal of Memory and Language 143 (2025) / arXiv:2502.20469** — Pair it immediately with the previous paper: humans reliably treat probable, improbable, impossible and inconceivable as distinct categories with distinct response-time signatures, and LM-derived probabilities can decode that structure. This is the human target your surprise metric is implicitly being compared against, and it is the ready-made stimulus taxonomy for the matched-novelty triad.  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20469
8. **Kang, Yue, Lu, Lin, Zhao, Wang, Huang & Feng, 'How Far is Video Generation from World Model: A Physical Law Perspective', arXiv:2411.02385 (ICML 2025); project page phyworld.github.io** — The mechanism paper. In a fully controlled 2D testbed it shows near-perfect in-distribution generalisation, real combinatorial scaling, and no OOD benefit — with generalisation that is case-based (nearest-training-example mimicry) and an attribute priority of colour > size > velocity > shape, i.e. a static appearance variable preserved at the cost of a dynamical one. Read the 'Open Discussion' section carefully: the pixel/VAE-latent-distance explanation is an unmeasured hypothesis and the ring control is presented by the authors as supporting it, not falsifying it.  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02385
9. **Vafa, Chang, Rambachan & Mullainathan, 'What Has a Foundation Model Found? Using Inductive Bias to Probe for World Models', arXiv:2507.06952** — Read this the moment you are tempted to put a probing result in the positive column. Models that predict orbital trajectories near-perfectly fail to apply Newtonian mechanics when adapted to new physics tasks, behaving as if they had learned task-specific heuristics — so decodability is not use. Also read its companion Myhill-Nerode paper (arXiv:2406.03689) and the 2026 tokenisation rebuttal, since the result is contested.  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06952
10. **Motamed, Culp, Swersky, Jaini & Geirhos, 'Do generative video models understand physical principles?' (Physics-IQ), arXiv:2501.09038 (WACV 2026); leaderboards at google-deepmind/physics-IQ-benchmark** — For the realism/physics dissociation and for a benchmark whose ceiling is the physical repeatability of the real world rather than a human judgement — Sora was simultaneously the most visually deceptive model and the worst on physics. It also gives you the cleanest reason prediction error is not a physics-sensitive quantity: if prediction quality is uncorrelated with physical correctness, error magnitude is dominated by appearance factors that vary independently of physics. Quote Original and Verified protocols separately.  
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09038
11. **Krojer, Komeili, Ross, Garrido, Sinha, Ballas & Assran, 'A Shortcut-aware Video-QA Benchmark for Physical Understanding via Minimal Video Pairs' (MVP), arXiv:2506.09987** — Read for the methodology of shortcut removal — minimal-change pairs that force a bias-driven model to 0% rather than to chance — and for the fact that FAIR built it because prior physical-understanding benchmarks are susceptible to score inflation from superficial cues. It is also the clearest instance of the conflation your paper targets: it takes IntPhys, InfLevel and GRASP violation stimuli and turns them into multiple-choice questions, so a low score cannot distinguish 'did not notice' from 'could not verbalise'.  
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09987
12. **Internò, Pondaven, Issa, Pizzati, Pinto, Olhofer, Laptev, Torr, Simoncelli, Hammer & Klindt, 'GeoPhys: The Geometry of Physical Plausibility', arXiv:2606.20707 / github.com/christianinterno/GeoPhys** — Read last, because it is the strongest threat to your own thesis: five training-free kinematic statistics on frozen non-video image features reportedly reach 93.3% on IntPhys 2 and 98.3% on LikePhys with no world model, no prediction and no physics. Either it replicates — in which case violation detection on current suites is a trajectory-smoothness heuristic and your deflationary argument becomes much stronger and much sharper — or it does not, and you should say why. Treat the near-chance baselines as an unverified competing-method claim until the code and tables are out.  
   https://github.com/christianinterno/GeoPhys
